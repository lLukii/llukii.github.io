It feels like yesterday when I walked through the red doors on 62nd street for the first time as a scrawny freshman. Now, I'm the elephant in the room—*what the actual fuck*. I think my past two blog posts were a bit too existential, so I'll lighten the mood up this time and just talk about what I've been up to. 

By far the best perk of being a senior is the extent of freedom at school. In previous years, I feel like I was forced to take a ton of classes which I barely had any interest in. Although most of my classes as a senior are half credit, I feel like I genuinely have time to explore my passions, even if they aren't dubbed the most rigorous courses at my school. Of course, I've only met with each class a few times, so my opinion is a bit skewed for now. I guess I'll still talk about my experience so far though. 
* English 12 (Required): Sadly no APs at my school, but we can choose from a list of 3-4 semester-long electives. I choose one about exploring cities/social constructs after destruction and how literature documents/erases their remnants—at least, that's my understanding of it. Interesting content, but the same teacher that threw a million socratic seminars at me last year, so idk how I'll do on those. 
* Introduction to Philosophy (1/2 Credit): This was BY FAR one of my best choices in high school. At my school this course is taught by the principal, and he makes the class super engaging and pretty fun. I had a gut feeling that I'd enjoy philosophy much more than another year of history, and I certainly made the right judgement. 
* Calc III (Dual Enrollment): It's alright. My prof is lowkey braindead because she just reads off the course textbook and tells us to memorize shit 💀 Thankfully attendance isn't recorded in my final grade so I usually just skip lectures and take notes asynchronously unless homework is due. 
* AP Stats: I *hate* this class; it's like watching paint dry, especially as an machine learning enthusiast—I want to drop it ASAP...
* AP Chemistry: I felt like my science courseload was a bit 1-dimensional with only physics, and I want to major in EE where chemistry knowledge would probably be very helpful. It's a nice class so far since there's only 8 people taking it, so my teacher is pretty responsive with questions and stuff. I did have a test the first week of school, but that turned out fine since Unit 1 isn't hard. 
* AP Physics C E&M (1/2 Credit): This is probably the most off-limits course at my school, so there's only 4 people—all the nerdy STEM seniors at Browning—taking it. I get along with the other three pretty well and the new physics teacher is really cool so that's a win-win. So far the content isn't that hard, but I guess that'll change later all. I will also say that my teacher is a LEGEND for using HRK :D 
* Data Science: A slightly harder version of AP stats probably, but still very straightforward for me as someone who grinds ML. MT is the absolute goat, but I'm not a fan of the class size or most people in the class. 
* Music Production (1/2 Credit): I'M ACTUALLY SO GLAD I TOOK THIS CLASS OMFG!!!! Like I always feel like I've been playing piano behind everyone's back, since virtually all my practicing is at home or at MSM. My teacher said that the class would perform in recitals and offered me to record one of my senior year pieces. This class is mentioned on instagram A LOT, so I'd be very happy if they posted an excerpt some time later of me playing one of my pieces—I just need finish learning my Beethoven Sonata. 
* Storytelling (Required, 1/2 Credit): I. Hate. This. Class. Already. It's not a secret that I'm not the best public speaker, but I basically don't know anyone in my section. The annoying thing is I probably have to present an anecdote, but I'm not sure how comfortable I am with disclosing anything beyond trivial stuff in my life to them. Come on, I already had a public speaking class in freshman year. Private schools really add the dumbest electives to sound special...

On the topic of stats, there's actually a pretty good chance I'm able to drop it. If you're an avid reader of my blog posts, you might remember me contemplating about my science fair project and how I'll pull through no matter what. Turns out, working my literal ass off in August paid off, as MT offered to open a class just for me where she'd help me publish my work in IEEE after reading my paper. I also finished it in time for the S.T. Yau Science competition, but results aren't out until October. I guess I'll update you guys on how that goes if I win something, but I'm not too sure since it's pretty competitive. 

College apps, however, are really starting to get to me. To be fair, my junior year summer was by far my busiest but also most rewarding, as I spent 8 weeks interning at a robotics/biomed lab while working on my side research project. However, both of these activities took an insane amount of time out of my day, so my college essays are... chopped. I wrote a "first attempt" version of my personal statement in July, but I kind of hit a brick wall after that after I realized that my topic was pretty generic and not representative of my life journey. I honestly can't believe that, after all this time, it's my turn to face the dreaded common app and pray to the lord that the AOs don't completely fuck me over. Of course, not going to a t20 school is by no means death sentence, but finally seeing how my high school activities would determine what university I end up at is, indeed, a bit scary. I guess I really need to calm down though; like I've said many times before, I'm sure that things will work out either way. 

I guess I'll update you on how things turn out :)
