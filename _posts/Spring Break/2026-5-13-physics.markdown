**2024 F=MA #8:**
Let's bash this problem with coordinates. At any given time, the rightmost point is $(0,x)$ for some $x$, and the topmost point is $(\sqrt{L^2-x^2},0)$. Thus, the midpoint can be expressed as

$$p=\Big(\frac{x}{2},\frac{\sqrt{L^2-x^2}}{2}\Big)$$

Let $\frac{dx}{dt}=v$. The velocity vector is therefore 
$$p'=\Big(\frac{1}{2}v,\frac{-x}{2\sqrt{L^2-x^2}}v\Big)$$

At $45^\circ$, $x=\frac{L}{\sqrt{2}}$. Taking the magnitude and substituting gives us

$$v_2=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}v^2+\Big(\frac{-L/\sqrt 2}{2\sqrt{L^2/2}}v\Big)^2}$$
$$=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}v^2+\frac{1}{4}v^2}=\boxed{(B) \frac{v}{\sqrt 2}}$$

**MIT 8.01 PSET 5**
First, we know that two forces act on the block: gravity, and tension from the string. Since the block is on an incline, gravity is $mg\sin\theta$, and we can label the tension force as $T$. 

$$ma=mg\sin\theta-T$$

Tension is what causes torque in the pulley, which moves in tandem with the sliding block. 

$$T=\frac{I\alpha}{R}=\frac{1/2 m_PR^2 (\frac{a}{R})}{R}=\frac{1}{2}m_pa$$

Substituting tension into eq. 1 gives us 
$$ma=mg\sin\theta-\frac{1}{2}m_pa$$
$$\implies a=\frac{mg\sin\theta}{m+\frac{1}{2}m_P}$$

Since the block is initially at rest, we know that $\frac{1}{2}at^2=d$ so $t=\sqrt{\frac{2d}{a}}$. Substituting $a$ from eq. 2 gives us our final answer: 

$$t=\sqrt{\frac{2d(m+\frac{1}{2}m_P)}{mg\sin\theta}}$$

**HRK Chapter 17 P3**
Block 2 slips if and only if the force between the blocks exceeds maximum static friction, or $\mu mg$. If we treat the blocks as a rigid body, the net force exerted on them is $F=(m+M)a$, so the force between them is $F_{1,2}=(\frac{m}{m+M})F$. Since a spring pulls both blocks, we can substitue $F$ as $kx$ by Hooke's Law. 

$$\Big(\frac{m}{m+M}\Big) kx = \mu mg$$
$$\implies x=\frac{\mu mg}{k}\Big(\frac{m}{m+M}\Big)$$

Substituting the values given, we have 
$$x=\frac{0.42(1.22)(9.8)}{344}\Big(\frac{1.22}{9.95}\Big) \approx 0.119$$
